    What these numbers can't tell you
    • The July game only asked people with jobs. If you're retired or a student, "people like you" isn't in it.
    • It's what people said they did, not measured use.
    • Each number is an estimate. The shaded range is where the real number most likely falls (Epoch's 90% range).
    • "Too close to call" is a real test, not whether two bars overlap: a gap counts only when it's bigger than 1.645 times the combined standard error of the two groups.
    • Small groups have wide ranges. "Less than high school" is 68 people.
    Why a guessing game?
    • Guessing first, then seeing the gap, helped people remember and understand the data better, even people who knew little about the topic. Kim, Reinecke and Hullman, "Explaining the Gap", CHI 2017.
    • Grids of 100 people help readers with low numeracy understand risk. Galesic, Garcia-Retamero and Gigerenzer, "Using icon arrays to communicate medical risks", Health Psychology, 2009.
    • Counting dots beats reading a range for everyday decisions. Kay et al., "When (ish) is my bus?", CHI 2016.
