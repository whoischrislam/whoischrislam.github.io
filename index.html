The full arc

I started as a designer. Then I learned to code. Now I build the whole thing.

I studied Human-Computer Interaction and Human-Centered Design at UW and came out believing that good design isn't decoration. It's how you show people you actually thought about them. That belief took me through Amazon, Mozilla, TaskRabbit, Clover Health, Iodine/GoodRx, and other early-stage health and education products, often as one of the first designers in the building. I built design systems, clinical tools, and data stories that shipped real outcomes: ~7–8× ROI on nurse home visits, Webby-winning drug information, and research that showed up in federal drug-pricing debates.

Somewhere along the way I got tired of throwing designs over the wall, so I started coding my own work, moved into a software engineer role at Modus Create, and then built the first PlaySesh product end to end in Vanilla JS, Express, and Supabase before co-founding the company.

PlaySesh began as a way to attack loneliness through games, then evolved into real-time tools for indie game teams. That chapter is where I proved to myself that I could own the full stack of a product: research, UX, systems, and production code.

In 2026 I co-founded y30 with Vijay Sivaji. It is voice AI for elder care: conversation-first daily check-ins that help older adults feel less isolated, while giving caregivers and clinicians the longitudinal signal they actually need. I own the product, the UX, and the voice stack.

I thrive in the 0→1 window, and I'm at my best as a committed co-owner on something that matters, not a contractor cycling through tickets. Healthcare is the thread. Voice is the medium. Dignity is the constraint.

What I'm looking for

  • Senior product engineer / design engineer roles where I own a product surface end to end: talking to users, writing specs, designing the IA and interactions, and shipping the full-stack implementation.
  • Small, high-talent teams with fuzzy lines between roles, minimal process overhead, and a culture of strong written communication over constant meetings.
  • Complex SaaS, AI, or tools products where power users depend on the product every day — and where design is a shared responsibility across engineering, product, and design, not a handoff.

y30

Co-Founder & CPTO · since 2026

Voice AI for older adults and their caregivers. I play a hybrid product engineer / design engineer role: I define problems with caregivers and clinicians, write the specs, design the IA and interactions, and ship the implementation — LiveKit, Python, React, Supabase. I've spent years in the unglamorous parts of healthtech: call centers, care-coordination handoffs. The places where the gap between the system and the actual person is hardest to ignore. y30 is the layer that closes it, built to create useful caregiver signal, not just transcripts. The voice AI architecture includes an FSM safety engine with deterministic crisis response so the model never generates crisis language.

Healthcare is the thread. Voice is the medium. Dignity is the constraint.

The problem

Caregivers and clinicians are flying blind between appointments. A senior can decline significantly in days, but the system only finds out at the next visit — if at all. The signal exists; it just has no infrastructure to surface it.

What I built

  • FSM safety engine Crisis paths are deterministic code, not prompt instructions — a hard state the model cannot override. In healthcare, you cannot let an LLM improvise on a suicidal ideation turn.
  • Longitudinal memory Sessions accumulate into caregiver intelligence over time. One call is a transcript. Thirty calls is a cognitive and emotional baseline a clinician can actually act on.
  • Elder-first voice UX No app, no screen, no login — just a phone call. Every friction point removed for a 75+ user who may not own a smartphone or tolerate a new interface.

PlaySesh

Co-Founder & CEO · 2024–2026

A collaborative game-thinking layer for indie devs, built inside Discord. I started it solo, proved the concept, then recruited Brett Groshong and Lawrence Kwok as co-founders. What began as a play against loneliness through gaming evolved into a real-time design and playtesting tool for small game teams. 1,790 commits. Zero ad spend.

119 servers · 5,000+ users · 1,302 MAU · 50-min avg sessions — all organic.

The problem

Small indie game teams have no single source of truth. Design lives in Figma, decisions in Notion, code in the engine, and feedback scattered across Discord threads. PlaySesh is the layer that connects them — canvas, IDE companion, and playtesting in one loop.

What I built

  • Built inside Discord Game devs already live in Discord. Building as a Discord Activity meant zero cold-start adoption friction — the tool was already where the team was.
  • Solo 0→1 build Designed and shipped the first version alone — Vanilla JS, Express, Supabase — before recruiting co-founders. No design-to-engineering handoff because I was both. 1,790 commits and 291 passing tests is the proof.
  • Product-led pivot User behavior, not a roadmap, drove the shift from social gaming to game dev tooling. Sessions ran 50 minutes on average. That signal told us what people actually wanted to do.
